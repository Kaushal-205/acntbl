# Acntbl design review

Reviewed the [prototype at 6e41cc5](https://github.com/Kaushal-205/acntbl/tree/6e41cc53f27133bda44914d81cc9959bc97b41a6). This is a source and mechanism review. The examples below are design analyses. The existing CI run covers the original 11 tests. Market demand, pricing, and legal classification remain unvalidated.

## Decision

**Pilot a capped guarantee for one deterministic workflow. Defer real-money trading and external restaking until the guarantee works on its own.** The strongest part of Acntbl is that a provider can put capital behind a specific promise and a third party can independently check whether it kept that promise. The weakest part is that someone with a genuine loss can receive zero when reporting fails. A customer evaluating coverage will care more about that case than about a successful happy-path demonstration.

## What exactly is covered?

Consider an accounts-payable agent authorized to transfer 100 USDC to Alice. Its signed instruction names Alice, yet the settled transfer sends 100 USDC to Bob. A verifier can compare the instruction and receipt. Measuring the outstanding loss also requires finality, refunds, reversals, a valuation time and an attribution rule. If the operator approved Bob, the user supplied the wrong address, or Bob returned 80 USDC, the same outgoing transfer tells three different stories.

For the pilot, freeze the authorized instruction format, chain, token, execution identity, covered parties, recovery cutoff, exclusions and per-action limit before execution. Give the user a receipt identifying the applicable policy and reserved capacity. Define who receives later recoveries where compensation has already been paid. For a non-financial failure, sell a pre-agreed fixed benefit for an observable breach. Calling a severity amount an exact financial loss makes the promise harder to defend. A general hallucination detector cannot establish these facts by itself.

## Product and economic assumptions

| Assumption | Why it can fail | Decision or experiment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Providers will pay premiums | The provider bears the premium and first-loss bond while the user receives the benefit. Providers with higher expected losses may value coverage most. | Ask a design partner for a paid pilot and compare its quote with directly posting the same guarantee in escrow. Track whether coverage changes a buyer's adoption decision. |
| Stakers can earn sustainable yield | Premiums are voluntary transfers. Market fees left over after settlement go to a configured recipient. The contracts impose no premium at inception. Stakeholders can lock funds for a year and earn zero. | Require a funded premium and disclose capital, term, first-loss bond and maximum payout at subscription. Model expected underwriting losses, operating cost and capital cost. |
| A market prices insurance | A binary event probability says little about severity. Two agents with 1% failure probability and average loss of 100 versus 10,000 have very different expected claims. The vault never reads the market odds. | Quote frequency and severity separately using exposure data. Run a paper market and measure forecast accuracy against a simple historical baseline. |
| Participants can all earn | Winning traders receive losing traders' stakes after fees. Underwriters absorb losses beyond the reserve and bond. Funding market fees requires trading demand even for obscure agent-specific epochs. | Identify the recurring payer and counterparty for each reward. Model an entire epoch with zero trading volume and correlated claims across multiple agents. |
| A YES position provides useful protection | Entry is possible only before coverage starts. There is no exit trade or locked-in payoff quote. Final odds depend on later deposits. A small one-sided pool can display 100% YES. | Measure executable hedge size and payout dispersion. Show pot depth, stake concentration and closing time alongside implied odds. Keep this signal out of automatic underwriting until calibrated. |
| Restaking improves capital efficiency | Outside capital adds another claimant on the same security budget and another settlement clock. The same model provider, API or wallet dependency can fail across many agents at once. | Stress a shared-dependency outage and collateral depreciation. Compare recoverable dollars and settlement time with a segregated stablecoin vault. |
| Product classification is straightforward | Provider-funded guarantees, third-party underwriting and retail event trading create distinct questions about who may offer, buy and distribute each product. | Scope the first jurisdiction, customer and distribution channel. Obtain advice on each activity before a funded public launch. This review makes no jurisdiction-specific legal determination. |

## Contract and architecture findings

“Blocker” means the behavior can defeat the advertised compensation promise. “High” means it materially affects fairness, liveness or financial viability. Each finding names the code path so it can become an engineering ticket.

| Priority | Finding and consequence | Change and acceptance criterion |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Blocker | **A successful dispute can erase a genuine claim.** Only one report is accepted per epoch. Rejecting an incorrect victim list in an otherwise valid incident voids the entire epoch. Missing reports and arbitration timeouts also release principal. See [assertReport, finalize and arbitrate](../src/mocks/MockOracle.sol#L169). | Separate incident determination from claim allocation. Allow a corrected root in a new round and a bounded victim appeal before releasing reserved capital. Demonstrate recovery for a genuine victim omitted from the first report. |
| Blocker | **Coverage can start with almost no capacity.** A provider sets the cap independently of available assets. The vault can have zero deposits and the market zero volume. Registration requires only the deployment minimum bond. A cap of 1,000 with 25 funded can pay at most 25. See [openEpoch](../src/mocks/MockOracle.sol#L135). | Distinguish the policy limit from guaranteed capacity. Activate coverage only after reserving the promised amount or explicitly sell a pro-rata product. Show each customer their entitlement under simultaneous claims. |
| Blocker | **A valid signature and Merkle proof leave the hardest facts unresolved.** The chain checks signed amounts, root membership and a running ceiling. A quorum supplies the root and denominator. Missing victims cannot prove omission through the claim function. Inconsistent leaf totals can leave money stranded or make redemption depend on claim order. A hash alone gives challengers no data. See [_validateReport](../src/mocks/MockOracle.sol#L198) and [claim](../src/CompensationEscrow.sol#L37). | Require accessible versioned evidence and claim data before the challenge clock begins. Define inclusion appeals and validate aggregate sums and canonical IDs. Demonstrate that an independent party can rebuild the root and a victim can obtain a proof without the proposer. |
| High | **The penalty covers equivocation only.** A consistently false report signed once per attester, or silence during an incident, does not satisfy the double-sign rule. The mock arbitrator is one address. Neither participation nor independent observation is continuously funded. See [proveDoubleSign](../src/mocks/MockOracle.sol#L220). | Define signer and challenger compensation, fallback reporting, conflict rules and independent evidence sources. Compare enforceable collateral with the total economic exposure, including trading positions. |
| High | **Honest corrections conflict with slashing.** Two structurally valid reports for an epoch can differ solely in the evidence encoding. Signing both is punishable. Fixed membership has no exit or rotation. Enough unavailable or disqualified keys can void every subsequent epoch. | Canonicalize evidence, define mutually exclusive statements and explicit report rounds, snapshot membership and introduce delayed exits. Verify that replacements preserve accountability for past epochs. |
| High | **Premium allocation has no earning period.** Deposits receive shares one-for-one and split all funded premiums by final share count. An early participant can see an advertised premium diluted by deposits just before the cutoff. See [stake, fundPremium and redeem](../src/UnderwritingVault.sol#L34). | Close subscriptions before allocating a binding premium, or quote variable premium per share explicitly. Test two entry times and publish the exact economics each staker accepts. |
| High | **Market settlement depends on the same committee traders are betting on.** Providers, traders, attesters and challengers can overlap. Voiding a market returns gross stakes, so the participants' preferences over void, YES and NO can differ substantially. Oracle bond sizing is fixed independently of pool size. | Quantify the maximum benefit of influencing each outcome, cap open interest relative to economic security, and design incentives for active independent disputes. For the pilot, benchmark against running the guarantee without a tradable event. |
| High | **The slash hook assumes immediate spendable proceeds.** Settlement credits the escrow using the amount returned by the adapter in one transaction. External veto, expiry, partial recovery or collateral routed elsewhere can invalidate that assumption. See [_settle](../src/mocks/MockOracle.sol#L293) and [vault interface](../src/interfaces/IUnderwritingVault.sol). | Track requested, finalized and actually received amounts separately. Reconcile receipts by epoch before opening claims. Verify capture time, upstream deadline and destination on a fork of the chosen deployment. [Symbiotic slashing mechanics](https://docs.symbiotic.fi/learn/core-concepts/slashing) |
| High | **A single epoch report must represent every incident.** It has one exact amount or one scheduled severity. Two different severity events, or one financial loss plus one scheduled benefit, have no explicit item-level representation. An oracle submitting one aggregate loses the on-chain distinctions. | Specify per-incident identity, valuation and limits, then aggregate accepted claims per epoch. Exercise multiple incidents, partial recovery and duplicate submissions across overlapping policies. |
| High | **Credibility can be reset.** Registration creates a fresh score and accepts a self-declared risk tier. A provider can use another agent ID. Penalty size also depends on the provider-selected epoch cap. The same loss can produce different scores. See [register and settle](../src/AgentRegistry.sol#L33). | Bind operating identities and software versions to durable history. Publish verified volume, incidents, gross loss, recovery and payout alongside any score. Calibrate risk tiers independently before using them to price anything. |
| Medium | **Operational failures can freeze settlement.** The coordinator resolves the market, pays recipients, debits the bond, slashes, opens escrow and releases the vault atomically. A collateral transfer rejection can roll the entire sequence back. | Specify supported token behavior, isolate recipient withdrawals, and exercise settlement with paused collateral and an unavailable recipient. Preserve each beneficiary's accounting during recovery. |
| Medium | **Indexer semantics and residual assets need explicit handling.** EpochSettled names its last argument reportHash but emits evidenceHash. Its verifiedLoss field contains capped loss. Integer rounding can leave dust after all entitlements are claimed. | Emit the typed report digest, evidence hash, gross loss, cap and actual funding distinctly. Define a dust policy that preserves outstanding claims and prove accounting conservation across epochs. |

## Verification priorities

The [existing suite](../test/Accountability.t.sol) exercises a credible happy path, dispute rejection, timeout, double signing and bounded payout arithmetic. Its two fuzz tests vary amounts on a fixed sequence. The following are proposed checks.

1. **Entitlement under failure.** A valid victim remains eligible when the first root is incomplete, the reporter is offline or the original arbitrator misses its deadline. Specify the maximum time to compensation for each case.
2. **Solvency across epochs.** Stateful tests should interleave registration, trades, premiums, multiple concurrent agent pools, disputes, refunds and claims. Reconcile token balances against each pool's remaining liabilities and demonstrate that claim ordering preserves valid entitlements.
3. **Oracle independence.** Have two independently implemented verifiers replay the same evidence corpus. Include refunds, duplicate executions, wrong beneficiaries, authorized overrides and stale data. Record disagreement and manual adjudication time.
4. **Economic viability.** Quote one provider a guarantee with a stated cap and claim deadline. Test whether its paid premium supports capital cost, monitoring, dispute handling and expected losses under zero market volume. A subsidized positive-yield demo cannot answer this.
5. **Adapter recovery.** Exercise partial, delayed, vetoed and expired upstream slashes. Verify that each victim's stated payout is backed by assets the escrow actually received.

## Build order

Start with one provider, one stablecoin, one beneficiary-verification workflow and segregated collateral. Give each covered execution a receipt. Run a shadow claims process against historical and synthetic incidents and let someone outside the team reproduce each outcome. Move to a capped pilot when a provider accepts a priced quote, affected users can recover independently, and available funds cover the promise. Add a prediction market when it demonstrates useful forecasting or hedging at realistic depth. Add restaking when an adapter demonstrates better capital economics after accounting for collection time and shortfalls.
